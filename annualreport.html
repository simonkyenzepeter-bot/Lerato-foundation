FOREWORD

Lerato Foundation is committed to the safety, protection, and well-being of every child who comes into contact with our programmes. This Handbook provides clear guidance on our standards, expectations, and procedures for safeguarding children across all our initiatives, including the Darajani Sports Academy in Kajiado County and our community programmes across Kenya.

Every individual associated with Lerato Foundation—whether staff, volunteer, partner, or stakeholder—has a vital role to play in creating safe environments where children can learn, play, grow, and thrive. This Handbook equips you with the knowledge and tools to fulfil this responsibility.

We urge every reader to study this Handbook carefully, internalise its principles, and commit to upholding the highest standards of child protection in all interactions.

Together, we can protect every child.

Simon Kyenze Peter

Chief Executive Officer

Lerato Foundation

Section 1

Our Commitment

Lerato Foundation (formerly Unawiri Children Foundation) is a youth-led, student-centered, and community-driven organization committed to empowering young people and strengthening communities across Kenya. Founded in 2019 and headquartered in Nairobi, our programmes encompass education, mentorship, nutrition (A Meal A Day), clean water, sports, and community development.

The Darajani Sports Academy in Kajiado County serves as our flagship initiative, currently supporting 76 elite athletes aged 16–19 under Coach Natalie, with a nationwide scouting programme underway. The Academy was founded in 2020 as Darajani Football Club in Makueni County and has since evolved into a premier youth sports development project.

We are unwavering in our commitment to the safety, protection, and well-being of every child who comes into contact with our programmes, personnel, and partners. We recognise the inherent vulnerability of children and dedicate ourselves to creating environments that are safe, nurturing, and conducive to holistic development—where children can reach their full potential, free from all forms of abuse, neglect, exploitation, and harm.

Zero Tolerance Statement: We maintain a zero-tolerance policy towards any form of child abuse, neglect, exploitation, or harm. Any substantiated violation of this policy by any individual associated with the Foundation—whether staff, volunteer, partner, contractor, or stakeholder—will result in immediate disciplinary action, including termination of engagement and referral to relevant statutory authorities for criminal prosecution where applicable.

This commitment is founded on the conviction that the best interests of a child are of paramount importance in every matter concerning the child, as affirmed by the Constitution of Kenya, the Children Act, 2022, and the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child.

Section 3

Legal Framework

This Handbook is grounded in and informed by the following legal instruments:

3.1 International Frameworks

  • United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC) – Ratified by Kenya in 1990. Establishes the fundamental rights of children.
  • African Charter on the Rights and Welfare of the Child – Ratified by Kenya in 1992.

3.2 Kenyan Legislation

  • The Constitution of Kenya (2010), Article 53 – Enshrines the right of every child to protection from abuse, neglect, harmful cultural practices, and hazardous labour.
  • The Children Act, 2022 – The primary legislation governing child protection in Kenya.
  • The Sexual Offences Act (2006) – Criminalises various forms of sexual abuse and exploitation.
  • The Counter-Trafficking in Persons Act (2010) – Prohibits trafficking of persons, including children.
  • The Data Protection Act (2019) – Governs the collection, processing, and storage of personal data.
  • The Penal Code – Criminalises acts of physical harm, assault, and abuse against children.

3.3 National Policies

  • Children (Care and Protection) Regulations, 2024
  • Framework for the National Child Protection System for Kenya
  • Child Protection in Emergencies: Operational Guidelines
Section 4

Key Definitions

Understanding the following definitions is essential for all personnel:

TermDefinition
ChildAny person under the age of 18 years, as defined by the Kenyan Constitution, the Children Act, 2022, and the UNCRC.
Child ProtectionActions taken in response to identified or suspected abuse, exploitation, or neglect. Includes both prevention and responsive measures.
Child SafeguardingA broader concept than child protection, encompassing proactive measures to promote children's well-being and protect them from harm.
Child AbuseEncompasses four main forms: physical abuse, sexual abuse, emotional/psychological abuse, and neglect.
Physical AbuseAny non-accidental physical injury, including beating, slapping, kicking, biting, burning, pushing, shoving, shaking, or inappropriate restraint.
Sexual AbuseAny abuse of a child of a sexual nature, including physical sexual contact and non-contact sexual exposure.
Emotional/Psychological AbusePersistent behaviour that undermines a child's self-esteem and emotional well-being.
NeglectThe persistent failure to meet a child's basic physical and emotional needs.
ExploitationUsing a child for economic, labour, sexual, or other harmful purposes.
GroomingThe deliberate process by which an adult builds a relationship with a child for the purpose of sexually abusing or exploiting them.
Corporal PunishmentAny punishment in which physical force is used and intended to cause some degree of pain or discomfort. Expressly prohibited under Section 22 of the Children Act, 2022.
Designated Child Protection Officer (DCPO)The person appointed by Lerato Foundation to act as the primary contact for all safeguarding concerns.
Vulnerable ChildrenChildren at heightened risk of abuse, including children with disabilities, children from ethnic minority communities, children in rural areas or slum dwellings, refugees, orphaned children, and children in residential care.

Important Note: These definitions are guided by Kenyan law, including the Children Act, 2022, and the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child (UNCRC).

Section 6

Prevention Strategies

Prevention is the cornerstone of effective child protection. Lerato Foundation shall implement comprehensive measures to prevent harm before it occurs.

6.1 Safe Recruitment and Screening

Rigorous screening practices shall be implemented to ensure that all personnel are suitable to work with children.

  • Application & InterviewComprehensive application and formal interview
  • Reference ChecksAt least two professional reference checks
  • Police ClearanceRecent certificate from DCI mandatory
  • Probationary PeriodAt least three months with close monitoring
  • ProhibitionIndividuals with criminal record involving children shall not be employed

6.2 Safe Online Practices

Given the increasing use of technology, robust measures shall be implemented to protect children in online spaces.

  • Staff Guidelines: Staff shall not share personal contact details with children online, add or accept children on personal social media accounts, send private messages to children through personal devices, communicate with children late at night, or share inappropriate digital content with children.
  • Child Education: The Foundation will educate children on how to stay safe online, teach them to recognise and report inappropriate behaviour, and conduct regular awareness sessions on digital safety.
  • Parent Engagement: Parents and guardians shall be informed of the Foundation's online safety measures. Signed consent shall be obtained for any online activities involving children.

6.3 Risk Assessment

All programmes, events, and activities shall be subject to a comprehensive risk assessment to identify potential risks to children and ensure adequate mitigation measures are in place.

6.4 Partnership Safeguarding

All partner organisations, contractors, and collaborators shall be required to demonstrate compliance with child protection standards.

Key Principle: Prevention is better than cure. By implementing robust prevention strategies, Lerato Foundation creates safe environments where children can thrive.

Section 7

Code of Conduct and Prohibited Behaviours

7.1 Professional Standards

Every member of staff, volunteer, coach, mentor, intern, consultant, contractor, and partner representing Lerato Foundation shall conduct themselves with the highest levels of integrity, professionalism, and respect. Personnel must always place the safety, dignity, and best interests of the child above personal or organisational interests.

7.2 Prohibited Behaviours

A. Inappropriate Relationships and Grooming

  • Staff shall not develop personal, romantic, or intimate relationships with any child.
  • Staff shall not groom, manipulate, or exploit a child for personal, emotional, or sexual benefit.
  • Staff shall not show favouritism toward any child.
  • Staff shall not spend unnecessary or prolonged time alone with a child in a private or unobserved setting.

B. Communication and Digital Conduct

  • Staff shall not contact children using personal mobile phone numbers, emails, or messaging applications for non-official purposes.
  • Staff shall not add or accept children on personal social media accounts.
  • Staff shall not send private messages, voice notes, or video calls to children through personal devices.
  • Staff shall not communicate with children late at night (after 8:00 PM).

C. Photography, Videography, and Media

  • Staff shall not take selfies or group photos with children using personal mobile phones.
  • Staff shall not share photographs, videos, or any identifiable images of children on personal social media accounts.
  • Staff shall not publish children's personal information on any public platform.

Zero Tolerance: Any violation of these standards will result in immediate disciplinary action, including dismissal and referral to authorities.

Section 8

Reporting and Response Procedures

8.1 Duty to Report

All staff, volunteers, and partners have a moral, professional, and legal obligation to report any suspicion or allegation of child abuse, neglect, or exploitation. This includes reporting historical abuse.

Key Principles for Reporting: The safety of the child is the primary concern. All reports shall be taken seriously. Reports shall be handled with sensitivity and confidentiality. No one shall be penalised for reporting in good faith.

8.2 How to Report

Internal Reporting: Reports shall be made immediately to the Designated Child Protection Officer (DCPO) at Lerato Foundation.

Section 9

Roles and Responsibilities

9.1 Board of Governors

  • Provides strategic oversight and ensures safeguarding policies align with the organisation's mission
  • Approves and reviews safeguarding policies periodically (at least every three years)
  • Ensures adequate resources are allocated for safeguarding initiatives
  • Holds the Chief Executive Officer accountable for implementing safeguarding measures
  • Reviews safeguarding reports

9.2 Chief Executive Officer

  • Ensures safeguarding policies are operationalised and embedded within the organisation
  • Fosters a culture of child protection and safeguarding throughout the organisation
  • Oversees compliance with legal safeguarding obligations
  • Provides regular safeguarding reports to the Board
  • Appoints the Designated Child Protection Officer
  • Ensures adequate budget allocation for safeguarding activities

9.3 Designated Child Protection Officer (DCPO)

  • Acts as the primary contact for all safeguarding concerns
  • Receives and documents all reports of safeguarding concerns
  • Conducts safeguarding training and capacity building
  • Investigates safeguarding incidents and liaises with relevant authorities
  • Ensures safeguarding procedures are effectively implemented
  • Reports cases to statutory authorities
  • Maintains confidential records of all safeguarding concerns
  • Provides regular reports to the Chief Executive Officer

9.4 All Staff and Volunteers

  • Abide by the safeguarding policy and code of conduct
  • Report any safeguarding concerns immediately
  • Participate in training and awareness programmes
  • Model appropriate behaviour when working with children
  • Maintain professional boundaries at all times

9.5 Partners and Contractors

  • Comply with the Foundation's safeguarding standards as part of their contractual obligations
  • Ensure safeguarding measures are implemented in their operations
  • Report any safeguarding concerns to the DCPO
Section 10

Training and Awareness

10.1 Induction Training

All new staff and volunteers shall complete safeguarding training during their induction, covering:

  • The Child Protection Policy and Code of Conduct
  • Recognising signs and indicators of different types of abuse
  • Understanding their duty to report concerns or allegations
  • The Foundation's reporting and response procedures
  • Relevant legislation and legal requirements
  • Professional boundaries and appropriate conduct
  • The role of the DCPO

10.2 Ongoing Training

Regular seminars shall be held for existing staff using qualified resource persons to keep them abreast with prevention and recognition of child abuse, consequences of abusive behaviour, occasions that could promote abusive behaviour, updated reporting procedures and responsibilities, and changes in legislation and best practice.

10.3 Training Records

All training sessions shall be documented. Attendance records shall be maintained. Staff and volunteers shall sign to confirm they have received, understood, and agree to abide by the policy.

Section 11

Monitoring, Evaluation, and Review

11.1 Monitoring

Lerato Foundation is committed to monitoring and evaluating the implementation of this Handbook. The DCPO shall:

  • Track all reported safeguarding concerns and their outcomes
  • Ensure all documentation is up to date, filed, and kept safely
  • Foster a safe climate in the workplace whereby all concerned can implement the policy in a competent and compassionate manner
  • Keep abreast of any changes in international, national, or local issues that may impact the policy or its implementation
  • Monitor compliance with the policy across all programmes and activities

11.2 Evaluation

The Handbook shall be reviewed at least once every three years, whenever there are significant changes in legislation, following any significant safeguarding incident, based on feedback from staff, volunteers, and stakeholders, and in response to emerging risks and best practice developments.

11.3 Lessons Learned

The Foundation shall establish a process where lessons can be learned from reviewed cases and new steps introduced to improve safeguarding practices.

Section 12

Confidentiality and Data Protection

12.1 Confidentiality Obligations

All safeguarding concerns shall be handled with strict confidentiality. Information shall only be shared on a strict need-to-know basis, with the Designated Child Protection Officer, with statutory authorities as required by law, and with the Chief Executive Officer and Board as appropriate.

12.2 Data Protection

All personal data relating to children, including safeguarding records, shall be handled in compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act (2019). Records shall be stored securely, with access limited to authorised personnel. Consent shall be obtained for data collection and processing. Children and families have the right to access and correct their data.

Key Principle: The privacy and dignity of all individuals, especially children, is respected at all times.
